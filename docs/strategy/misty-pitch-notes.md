# Misty Pitch Notes

Working founder notes for explaining Misty clearly, creating urgency, and keeping the product grounded in a distinctive thesis.

> **Scope note:** These notes emphasize collaborative human-and-agent teams. The canonical broader product and market model—including solo use, informal groups, the initial student wedge, and product–market fit signals—is [Misty Product–Market Fit Thesis](product-market-fit-thesis.md).

Last updated: August 18, 2026

## The core idea

Misty makes forming a mixed human-and-agent team as easy as starting a group chat.

Today, starting a project means assembling a fragmented stack: create the chat, make the task board, share a drive, invite people, connect tools, open private AI chats, and repeatedly explain the project to every agent. The group does not begin with a working environment. It has to construct one.

Misty lets someone create a Space for an objective, invite the people and agents involved, and begin working in seconds. The Space is a persistent environment containing the participants, tools, artifacts, conversations, tasks, and deliberately permitted context that belong to that work.

The product is not simply an AI assistant, project manager, chat app, or bundle of utilities. It is the environment in which a temporary or permanent human-and-agent team comes into existence and operates.

## The shortest articulation

> Spin up a collaborative environment for people and agents in seconds.

Supporting line:

> A Misty Space brings the team, agents, conversations, files, tasks, browser, and tools for an objective into one persistent place.

More memorable formulation:

> Create a Space. Add people. Add agents. Start working.

## The category thesis

Software was designed around one of two assumptions:

1. Humans use tools together.
2. One human talks privately to one AI.

Neither model fits the next phase of work. Teams will increasingly include several people, personal agents, shared agents, specialist agents, and the tools and material they all need to coordinate around an objective.

The missing primitive is not a smarter chatbot. It is a shared environment built for mixed teams from the beginning.

Misty's thesis:

- Agents should be participants, not buttons hidden inside applications.
- People and agents need a shared place, not isolated conversations.
- Tools should be available inside the environment where the work is coordinated.
- Context should persist with the objective instead of being reconstructed for each prompt.
- Access should be explicit. Joining a Space does not mean receiving unrestricted access to a person's device or private material.
- Starting a new working group should take seconds, not a day of configuration.

## The three layers of Misty

### 1. Participants

A Space has people and agents as first-class members.

Each participant should have an identity, role, permissions, presence, history, and visible relationship to the work. Agents should not feel like magical commands. The group should understand which agent did what, what it used, and when a human decision is needed.

### 2. Collaborative tools and surfaces

The environment contains the surfaces where work happens: conversations, planning, shared material, files, browser, code, terminal, and future extensions.

Misty should not merely point at work stored elsewhere. It should let the group open the relevant surfaces together and arrange them around the objective. The docking system is strategically important because it makes the environment composable rather than forcing every kind of work through a document or chat thread.

### 3. Persistent, permissioned context

The Space remembers the material and history that belong to the objective. Agents work from the context they are permitted to use. Private files and personal activity remain outside that boundary until someone deliberately brings them in.

This turns context from something users repeatedly package into prompts into part of the environment itself.

## What makes Misty different

The differentiation is not any single feature. It is the combination:

- An environment can be created instantly around an objective.
- People and agents occupy the same collaboration model.
- The actual working tools live beside the coordination layer.
- The Space persists after an individual chat or agent run ends.
- Context and permissions attach to the Space.
- Local and private material has a deliberate boundary from shared material.
- The environment can be rearranged around the work instead of imposing one universal document, board, or chat layout.

Traditional collaboration suites mostly centralize records of work. Agent products mostly centralize conversations with AI. Misty should centralize the living environment in which the work is understood, performed, reviewed, and handed off.

## What Misty is not

- Not an "everything app."
- Not a collection of unrelated tools inside one window.
- Not another project-management database with AI added.
- Not a chatbot with shared folders.
- Not an agent orchestration dashboard intended only for developers.
- Not a virtual machine or remote desktop with collaboration bolted on.
- Not a promise to replace every specialized application.

The goal is not to own every tool. The goal is to own the environment that gives participants and tools a coherent place to work together.

## The strongest positioning routes

### Recommended: instant environment

> Spin up a collaborative environment for people and agents in seconds.

Why it works:

- It describes a visible product action.
- Speed makes the promise concrete.
- "Environment" is larger than a chat or project board without relying on "operating system."
- It naturally introduces Spaces.
- The claim can be proven in a short demo.

What must be true:

- Creating a Space is genuinely fast.
- The initial state feels useful rather than empty.
- Inviting a person or agent is obvious.
- A new user can understand the Space without configuration.
- The first collaborative outcome happens within minutes.

### Alternative: the collaborative computer

> A shared computer for people and agents.

Why it works:

- It makes browser, files, code, terminal, and dockable surfaces feel essential rather than incidental.
- It differentiates Misty from document- and board-centered products.
- It suggests an environment capable of real action.

Risks:

- People may interpret it as screen sharing, a cloud desktop, or simultaneous control of one machine.
- It requires the tool surfaces and collaboration behavior to feel exceptionally coherent.

### Alternative: multiplayer AI

> Move AI out of private chats and into the team.

Why it works:

- The problem is immediately recognizable.
- It creates a clean before-and-after story.
- It frames agents as collaborators rather than personal assistants.

Risks:

- The phrase and concept are becoming crowded.
- It underplays Misty's environment and tool system.

### Alternative: private-by-default context

> Give agents the context they need without giving away everything else.

Why it works:

- It addresses a real trust barrier.
- Misty's local/private and Space/shared boundary can make the promise tangible.
- It is more credible than vague claims about "secure AI."

Risks:

- It is a reason to trust Misty, not necessarily a reason to desire it.
- It is strongest as supporting proof rather than the hero message.

### Avoid as the primary category: human-agent operating system

The phrase is ambitious but crowded, abstract, and easy for large work-management platforms to claim. It can describe the long-term vision internally, but it should not carry the external pitch.

## The FOMO story

Do not manufacture scarcity. The real urgency is a change in how quickly groups can form and operate.

The old workflow:

1. An idea appears.
2. Someone assembles applications and permissions.
3. People accumulate separate copies of the context.
4. Everyone opens private AI conversations.
5. Decisions and agent output become invisible to the rest of the group.
6. The team spends increasing energy synchronizing itself.

The Misty workflow:

1. An idea appears.
2. Someone creates a Space.
3. The people and agents join.
4. The environment already has the collaborative surfaces and permitted context.
5. Work begins and remains visible to the group.
6. The Space becomes more useful as its shared history accumulates.

The fear of missing out is not "you do not have AI." Everyone will have AI. It is:

> Other groups can now form, orient themselves, and begin executing before yours has finished assembling its stack and briefing its assistants.

Strong urgency lines:

- Every team will have agents. Very few will give them a real place to work.
- The bottleneck is moving from intelligence to coordination.
- AI made execution faster, but it made fragmented teamwork more visible.
- A private AI chat makes one person faster. A shared environment makes the group faster.
- The next high-leverage teams will be assembled around objectives, not software subscriptions.
- Do not add agents to a fragmented stack. Give the whole team an environment.

## Why now

- Agents are becoming capable of using browsers, files, code, terminals, and connected services.
- Individuals are adopting agents faster than organizations can coordinate their use.
- Agent output is increasingly real work, yet it remains trapped in private sessions and product-specific histories.
- Teams need visibility, permissions, continuity, and handoffs between people and agents.
- Existing collaboration products were built around human-generated documents, messages, records, and tasks. Adding an agent does not automatically redesign their underlying collaboration model.
- The cost of creating software environments is falling, making it possible for a Space to feel complete immediately rather than beginning as an empty container.

Misty lets you spin up a complete collaborative environment for the people and agents working on an objective in seconds. Each Space keeps the team, tools, shared material, and permitted context together, so the group can start working immediately and agents do not have to be re-briefed in isolated chats.

> Imagine creating a group chat, except the room comes with the people, agents, shared files, tasks, browser, code, terminal, and whatever tools that objective needs. Everyone joins the same persistent environment, agents understand the context they are permitted to use, and work can move between humans and agents without disappearing into private threads. That is a Misty Space, and you can create one in seconds.

## A venture-scale pitch

> Work is shifting from software used only by people to environments occupied by people and agents. Existing suites are adding AI features to applications designed around documents, tasks, or chat. Misty starts from a different primitive: the Space, a persistent environment created around an objective where humans, agents, tools, context, and permissions come together. If mixed teams become the default unit of work, the environment that forms and coordinates those teams becomes a new platform layer.

## A user-facing pitch

> Create one Space for the thing you are trying to accomplish. Invite the people involved, add the agents that can help, bring in only the files and context they need, and work with your browser, tasks, conversations, and tools together. No setup marathon and no repeating the project in every AI chat.

## Demo story

The demo should make the thesis self-evident in roughly 90 seconds.

### Opening

Start with an objective, not the product interface:

> We are launching a small product next Friday.

### Sequence

1. Create a Space named after the launch.
2. Invite a collaborator.
3. Add or activate a relevant agent.
4. Bring a launch brief or selected local material into the Space.
5. Show the agent answering from permitted Space context.
6. Open the cited material beside the conversation.
7. Turn an unresolved decision into a task or update an existing task.
8. Open the browser, file, or code surface needed to continue the work without leaving the environment.
9. Show that the collaborator sees the shared result and can take over.

### Closing line

> In the time it normally takes to create the project folder, this team has an environment, shared context, and its first piece of work in motion.

The demo should avoid touring navigation. It should tell one uninterrupted story of a group forming and accomplishing something.

## The product must prove the pitch

### Instant formation

- A Space should be useful immediately.
- Templates should describe objectives, not software configurations.
- Inviting humans and agents should feel like the same conceptual action.
- The product should visibly measure time to readiness in seconds.

### Shared agency

- Agent work should be visible and attributable.
- The group should know when an agent is working, waiting, blocked, or requesting approval.
- Handoffs between people and agents should preserve context.
- Agents should leave durable work behind, not merely prose in a chat.

### A coherent environment

- Dockable tools should feel like parts of one environment, not embedded miniature applications.
- Opening a tool should preserve the Space and objective around it.
- Shared state and personal state need clear visual boundaries.
- A person should always understand what the group shares and what remains private.

### Continuity

- Closing the application must not erase the meaning of the environment.
- Returning to a Space should restore the work, participants, open questions, and useful arrangement.
- Agent runs should connect to artifacts, tasks, decisions, and approvals.

## The initial wedge

The platform vision can serve any group, but the first pitch should name a group with acute pain.

### Strongest initial wedge: small teams shipping digital projects

Examples include founder teams, product teams, agencies, research groups, and technical creative teams.

Why this wedge fits:

- They already jump among browser, files, code, terminal, chat, and tasks.
- They are early adopters of agents.
- They form temporary teams around objectives frequently.
- They feel both tool fragmentation and context repetition.
- Misty's current surfaces make the value visually demonstrable.

Possible wedge line:

> Misty gives small teams an instant workspace where people and agents can take a digital project from idea to shipped work.

### Broader future

The same primitive can support a family planning a trip, a study group, a community, an operations team, or a temporary response team. These examples demonstrate that Spaces are general, but including all of them in the opening pitch may make the product sound unfocused.

## Competitive framing without naming competitors

There are four broad categories:

1. Work-management suites organize tasks and records.
2. Knowledge tools organize documents and company information.
3. Communication tools organize messages.
4. Agent products organize private conversations or automated runs.

Misty's claim is that mixed teams need an environment encompassing participants, tools, context, and action—not another isolated record type.

Do not claim that competitors lack AI, agents, search, permissions, shared context, or integrations. Those claims will age badly. Emphasize the product architecture and experience:

- Misty begins with the environment.
- The environment is created around an objective.
- It includes working surfaces, not only records and automations.
- Humans and agents belong to the same participation model.
- The private/shared boundary is part of the core interaction.
- Formation speed is a product feature, not just onboarding copy.

## Defensibility

The moat cannot be a sidebar containing many tools. Any company can reproduce that screenshot.

Potential defensibility comes from:

- The graph connecting participants, permissions, conversations, tasks, artifacts, tool state, agent actions, and decisions inside each Space.
- A collaboration and runtime model that allows different agents and tools to participate coherently.
- Durable history and trust: who did what, using which context, with whose approval.
- A precise local/private/shared data boundary.
- An ecosystem of extensions, agents, and Space templates.
- Habit and accumulated context inside long-lived Spaces.
- Exceptional interaction design for switching between coordinating work and performing it.

The product should compound in value because the environment becomes more coherent, not merely because it stores more data.

## Likely objections

### "Isn't this just another all-in-one app?"

> An all-in-one app tries to replace every specialized tool. Misty gives people, agents, and tools a shared environment. Specialized tools can remain specialized; Misty coordinates the objective, context, access, and handoffs around them.

### "Why won't existing collaboration suites build this?"

> They can and will add agents. The bet is that products designed around documents, tasks, or chat will continue treating agents as features inside those objects. Misty is designed around mixed participation and a composable working environment from the beginning.

This answer only becomes convincing if the product visibly behaves differently.

### "Why does this need to be a desktop app?"

> The desktop is where private files, browsers, code, terminals, and native tools already live. It gives Misty a natural way to connect a person's actual work with a shared Space while maintaining an explicit boundary between local and shared material.

### "Does every teammate see my entire computer?"

> No. A Space contains what participants deliberately add or permit. Local and private work remains outside the Space by default.

### "Why not just invite an agent into chat?"

> Chat is one coordination surface. Real work also involves artifacts, tasks, tools, decisions, approvals, and state that must survive the conversation. Misty gives the agent a place in that larger environment.

### "Who controls the agents?"

> Agents operate through visible identities, scoped permissions, explicit tool access, and human approvals where required. Their work should be attributable and reviewable.

## Metrics that validate the story

- Median time from account creation to first Space.
- Median time from Space creation to first invited participant.
- Median time from Space creation to first useful agent result.
- Percentage of active Spaces containing at least two humans and one agent.
- Percentage of agent outputs connected to a durable artifact, task, decision, or action.
- Invite acceptance and collaborator activation rates.
- Weekly number of human-agent handoffs per active Space.
- Percentage of returning users who resume an existing Space instead of creating isolated sessions.
- Retention difference between solo Spaces and mixed human-agent Spaces.
- How often private material is deliberately promoted into shared Space context.

## Important open product questions

- What exactly is shared live between participants, and what is only personal presentation state?
- Can two people interact with the same browser, document, or terminal surface? If not, how is ownership communicated?
- What does it mean for an agent to be a member rather than merely callable?
- Which agent actions require approval, and how are approvals shown to the group?
- How does an agent cite or reveal the context behind an action?
- What durable objects do agent runs create besides messages?
- Can a Space be created from a goal and intelligently choose an initial environment?
- How do Space templates remain useful without turning into configuration-heavy project templates?
- What is the smallest magical collaborative loop the product can make flawless?
- Which parts of the environment work offline or locally?
- How are external agents brought into the same identity, permission, and activity model?

## The standard for every product decision

Ask four questions:

1. Does this make it faster to form the group around an objective?
2. Does this help people and agents work together rather than in parallel isolation?
3. Does this make the environment more coherent instead of adding another disconnected feature?
4. Does the user understand what is shared, what is private, and who can act?

If a feature does not strengthen one of these properties, it may not belong in the core Misty experience.
